What it does

Lyrics that keep time
Lines come from LRCLIB and run on a local clock, so the highlight moves smoothly instead of jumping each time Spotify is asked where you are. Two keys nudge the timing if your headphones lag.
Colour taken from the cover
The text colour is picked out of the album's own palette and checked for contrast against what is actually behind it. A purple record gives pale lavender, a red one warm cream. Never a clashing opposite.
Out of the way by default
No taskbar window and no focus stealing. It lives in the tray, stays above fullscreen games, and can hide itself until you press a key.
Yours to arrange
Two fullscreen views, five type pairings, any monitor, and a widget you can drag wherever it suits you.

Setting it up

  1. Download and run it

    Windows will show a blue “Windows protected your PC” panel, because the file is not signed by a certificate authority. Choose More info, then Run anyway. The installer needs no administrator rights.

  2. Register a Spotify app

    Syncity opens its settings on first run and walks you through this. Create an app at developer.spotify.com/dashboard, name it anything, tick Web API, and add http://127.0.0.1:8888/callback to its redirect URIs. Then copy the app's Client ID into Syncity. Three minutes, free, once.

  3. Connect

    Press Connect Spotify. Your browser opens once, you approve it, and the tab closes itself. Syncity then lives in the tray — the icon beside the clock.

Why you register your own app

Spotify lets an app that hasn't been through its review admit only the 25 people its owner has entered by hand in a dashboard. So there is no single app that could cover everyone who downloads this — shipping one would just fail for you on the first press.

Registering your own is better anyway: your own rate limit rather than one shared with every other listener, and your playback only ever travelling between your computer and Spotify. A Client ID is a public identifier, not a password — Syncity signs in with PKCE, which has no secret to store.

Questions

Why do I have to make a Spotify app?
Because Spotify won't let one app serve everyone. An unreviewed app admits 25 hand-entered users, so the only workable answer is that each person registers their own. Syncity walks you through it on first run, and you never touch it again.
Do I need Spotify Premium?
Only to skip and pause from the widget. Seeing what is playing, with lyrics, works on a free account.
Why does Windows warn me about it?
The builds are unsigned. A code-signing certificate is a yearly purchase, and this is a free app. The source is on GitHub if you would rather build it yourself.
Installer or portable?
The installer adds a Start Menu entry and an uninstaller. The portable build is one file that installs nothing. Both keep their settings in %APPDATA%\Syncity, so signing in once is enough either way.
A song has no lyrics.
Lyrics come from LRCLIB, a community database. Not every track is in it, and the overlay says so rather than sitting blank.
Mac or Linux?
Not yet. The build targets exist but nothing has been tested there, and the overlay leans on Windows-specific window behaviour.
